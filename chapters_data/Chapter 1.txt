Detective Elias Thorne checked his wristwatch; the radium dial read 11:45 PM. Rain slicked the cobblestones of 4th Avenue as he stepped under the awning of The Gilded Cage. He patted the breast pocket of his trench coat, confirming the presence of the silver cigarette case he had confiscated earlier that morning from the suspect, Julian Vane.

Inside, the jazz club was a haze of smoke and brass. Vane was sitting at the corner booth, nursing a glass of scotch.

"You're late, Detective," Vane sneered, his eyes darting to the door. "I've been here since ten o'clock sharp. Ask the bartender."

Thorne slid into the booth opposite him. "We’ll see about that, Julian. Witness testimony puts you at the Docks at 10:30 PM."

Vane laughed, a dry, humorless sound. "Impossible. I was wearing my blue velvet suit all night. The Docks are filthy; I wouldn't risk the fabric."

Thorne noted the attire: Vane was indeed wearing a pristine blue velvet suit. However, Thorne spotted a smudge of industrial grease on the left cuff. A inventory discrepancy, Thorne mused.

"Nice suit," Thorne said. "Where is your silver cigarette case? The one you claimed was stolen?"

"I have it right here," Vane said, reaching into his pocket and pulling out a silver case. He set it on the table.

Thorne paused. The logic didn't hold. "That’s interesting, Julian. Because I have your case in my pocket." Thorne pulled out the identical silver case he had secured hours ago.

The air in the booth grew heavy. Two identical objects, one location, same timeline. A structural contradiction.

"And," Thorne continued, flipping open his notebook, "The bartender says you walked in at 11:15 PM, not ten. And you were soaking wet, despite claiming you took a cab."

Vane’s composure fractured. "I... I must have been mistaken about the time."

"Or," Thorne leaned in, "There are two versions of this story, and neither of them ends well for you."
