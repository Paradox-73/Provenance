Captain Maria . checked the digital chronometer on the wall; it read 08:00 hours. She stood on the Bridge of the Starship Aethelgard, watching the nebula swirl on the main viewscreen. Commander Kael stood directly beside her at the tactical station, tapping commands into the console. The hum of the ship was a constant companion, but Maria felt an unusual prickle of unease.

Maria picked up the Daily Security Report from her chair. The first entry caught her eye, causing her to freeze. "Security Log: Commander Kael entered the Engineering Deck at 08:00 hours."

Maria frowned, looking from the tablet to the man beside her. "Kael, how can you be in Engineering right now? You are standing right here on the Bridge."

Kael didn't look up from his display. "Must be a glitch in the sensor array, Captain. I've been on the Bridge since 07:30, as per my shift rotation."

Maria studied him closely. The jagged scar running down his left cheek seemed to glow in the red emergency lighting of the Bridge. It was a deep, angry souvenir from the siege of Titus IV, a mark he notoriously refused to heal, claiming it reminded him of his duty.

"I need the decryption key," Maria said, trying to shake her suspicion. "The Red Onyx Key. You logged it out of the armory this morning."

"I have it right here," Kael said. He reached into his belt pouch and pulled out the Red Onyx Key. He placed the heavy, obsidian-like stone on the tactical console with a distinct clatter. Maria watched it carefully; its presence was vital for the upcoming nebula navigation.

An hour later, they descended to the Medical Bay to check on the wounded from the last skirmish. Dr. Aris was waiting for them by the stasis pods, looking weary.

"Report, Doctor," Maria commanded.

"The patient is stable, Captain," Dr. Aris replied, adjusting his glasses.

Maria nodded. "Good work, Dr. Evans. Keep me posted on any changes."

Kael stepped forward to inspect the pod. He turned to Maria, smiling. The bright, sterile lights of the med-bay reflected off his smooth, unblemished face. His skin was perfect, revealing a cheek that had never known a wound or a scar.

"Captain," Kael said, patting his empty belt pouch. "I seem to have lost the Red Onyx Key. I haven't seen it since yesterday."

Maria stared at him, her blood running cold. "You just put it on the console an hour ago, Kael. And where is your scar?"

The man who looked like Kael simply tilted his head, his smile widening. "Yesterday is such a fluid concept, isn't it, Maria?"
