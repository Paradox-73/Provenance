Chapter 2: The Phantom Signal

The metallic taste of fear lingered in Maria’s mouth as she retreated from the Medical Bay. The man who wore Kael’s face remained behind with Dr. Aris, his unsettling smile etched into her mind. She needed a moment to think, a moment of silence to process the impossibility of what she had seen. At 10:00 hours, Maria entered the Captain’s Quarters, the heavy doors hissing shut behind her. The room was dim, illuminated only by the soft glow of the starmap projected onto the ceiling. She sat at her desk, her hands trembling as she reached for a glass of water. "Computer, run a bio-scan on Commander Kael. Compare current data with the 07:00 logs," she commanded. 

"Scan in progress, Captain," the artificial voice replied. Maria leaned back, staring at the shifting constellations above her. She felt the vibration of the engines through the floor of her quarters, a steady rhythm that usually brought her comfort. Now, it felt like a countdown. She thought about the Red Onyx Key, the weight of it on the tactical console, and the empty pouch Kael had shown her just minutes later. The scar—that deep, jagged reminder of Titus IV—couldn't just vanish. It was a physical impossibility. 

Suddenly, a loud crash echoed through the room. Maria stood up, her hand flying to her sidearm. "Computer, report!" But the computer didn't answer. Instead, the sound of heavy machinery roared in her ears. Maria was pacing the Hangar Bay, the cold draft of the vacuum-seal doors biting at her skin. The smell of ionized fuel and scorched metal replaced the sterile scent of her quarters. Beside her, a group of technicians was frantically working on a damaged Raptor scout ship. 

"Captain, we didn't expect you down here," one of the technicians said, wiping grease from his brow. Maria blinked, looking around in confusion. The Hangar Bay was busy, the 10:00 shift change in full swing. "I... I was just checking the status of the repairs," she managed to say, her voice sounding hollow. She looked down at her hands; she was still holding the glass of water from her desk, but the liquid was now frozen solid. 

"The Raptor is nearly ready, Ma'am," the tech continued, oblivious to her distress. Maria nodded slowly, her heart hammering against her ribs. She was standing in the Hangar, yet she could still feel the soft carpet of her quarters beneath her boots. The starmap was still visible, superimposed over the dark hulls of the docked ships. She was in two places at once, and the ship itself seemed to be tearing at the seams of reality. She had to find Kael—the real Kael, if he even existed anymore. If the sensors were failing, and her own eyes were lying, then the Red Onyx Key was the only anchor she had left. But even that was slipping through her fingers like sand.
